What it shows

The point every packet passes through.

Per-app traffic, every destination with the domain name the app looked up, and every device on the network around you.

Live monitoring

Download and upload rates for every program, today's totals, session totals and a 7-day history.

  • Every remote endpoint per app: domain, owner, country and city
  • Connections view with internet, local and listening filters
  • Dot-matrix world map with arcs to every destination
  • Hourly usage for 14 days, daily for 90
  • Export usage and destinations as CSV, or everything as JSON

Devices on your LAN

Finds devices by ICMP/TCP probes and the ARP table, then names them with reverse DNS, NetBIOS and mDNS.

  • MAC maker from an offline 54k-entry OUI database
  • Device type: router, phone, computer, TV, printer, camera…
  • Alerts when a new device joins
  • Presence timeline: when each device joined and left
  • Whole-network traffic from the router's UPnP counters

Runs quietly

Closes to the system tray or macOS menu bar and keeps monitoring, with a mini panel of live speeds and the busiest apps.

  • Android home screen widget with live speeds
  • Optional start in the tray at login
  • Samples every 2 s while shown, every 6 s while hidden
  • Per-socket counters straight from the kernel
  • Keyboard shortcuts for every page
Security

Block a program with a click. Undo it just as easily.

Destinations are checked offline against free blocklists (abuse.ch Feodo and URLhaus, Spamhaus DROP, FireHOL level 1), refreshed daily. Alerts fire when a program connects for the first time.

On Android, an on-device VPN relays traffic on the phone itself, with no server, to show every app's connections and domain names and enforce firewall rules. Ad and tracker blocking there is off by default and uses the AdGuard DNS filter and StevenBlack's hosts.

Privacy policy

Your network stays yours.

MiddleMan has no account, no analytics, no ads, no tracking and no crash reporting. Its developer receives nothing from the app. This policy covers every version: Windows, macOS, Linux and Android, from GitHub, the Microsoft Store and Google Play. Effective 19 November 2026.

Kept on your device

  • Network usage per app: hourly for 14 days, daily for 90 days.
  • Destinations each app connected to, with domain names, owners and locations.
  • Devices found on your network, the names you give them and when they came and went.
  • Firewall rules, alerts, speed test results and your settings.
  • Downloaded blocklists and cached location lookups.

All of it lives in MiddleMan's data folder and goes when you uninstall the app. Exports (CSV, JSON) are written only where you save them.

Sent to other services

  • geojs.io receives the IP addresses of the servers your apps connect to, to find their country, city and owner; ipinfo.io (or api.ipify.org) is asked for your own public address and its location. Both stop when you turn off online lookups in Settings.
  • Threat lists are downloaded from abuse.ch, Spamhaus and FireHOL (on GitHub) while threat alerts are on. Nothing about your traffic is uploaded.
  • Ad and tracker lists (AdGuard DNS filter, StevenBlack's hosts) are downloaded while ad blocking is on, on Android from GitHub. The Google Play version has no ad blocking.
  • Update checks: desktop downloads ask api.github.com for the latest release once a day; switch it off in Settings. Store copies don't check.
  • Speed tests go to speed.cloudflare.com when you run one or schedule them.
  • Tools you run (ping, traceroute, port scan, DNS, WHOIS through whois.iana.org and the registry it names, TLS inspector, Wake on LAN) contact only the host you enter. Host names are resolved through your system's DNS.

Like any connection, these services see your IP address; their own privacy policies apply.

The on-device VPN (Android)

  • Runs only when you turn it on, after Android asks for your consent.
  • Relays traffic on the phone itself, straight to where each app was going. There is no VPN server and nothing passes through the developer.
  • Never decrypts or stores what apps send. It records which app connected where and the domain names it looked up, on the phone, to show you and to apply your firewall rules.
  • DNS queries go to your network's own DNS servers.

Permissions

  • Usage access (Android): per-app network totals.
  • Location (Android): Android shows Wi-Fi names and nearby networks only to apps with it. Your location is never stored or sent.
  • Notifications: alerts you choose, such as new apps, new devices and outages.
  • Local network (macOS): the device scan and discovery.
  • Administrator rights (desktops, optional): every program's connections, per-app bytes on Windows, the firewall.

Children, changes and contact

MiddleMan isn't directed at children and collects no data from anyone. Changes to this policy appear on this page with a new date, and in the site's history. Questions: open an issue on GitHub.
